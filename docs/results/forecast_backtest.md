# Forecast backtest

Generated by `python -m stockroom.forecast.train --backtest`. Do not edit by hand.

- **Origin:** 2016-04-24. Train through the origin, forecast the next 28 days (2016-04-25 to 2016-05-22).
- **Model:** one global LightGBM across all store-SKU series: Tweedie for the mean, quantile models for P10/P90. Every feature looks at least 28 days back, and prices are frozen at the origin. Boosting rounds (early stopping on the 28 days before the origin): mean 98, P10 53, P90 323.
- **Baseline:** the customer's current method, the average of the same weekday over the 4 weeks before the origin.
- **WAPE** = sum|actual - forecast| / sum actual (lower is better). **Bias** = sum forecast / sum actual - 1. **Below P10 / above P90** = share of actual days under the P10 or over the P90 forecast; a calibrated model has at most 10% each. Above-P90 is the one safety stock depends on.

**Headline:** at store-SKU-day level the model's WAPE is **3.6 pp lower** than the baseline (improvement +3.6 pp, 95% CI [+3.3, +4.0] pp, bootstrap over 12,194 store-SKU series; significant at 5%).

## Reading this honestly
- Demand exceeded the P90 on 11.7% of store-SKU-days (target 10%): the P90 is slightly low, so safety stock is slightly understated. Reorder safety stock uses the P90 side only.
- The model is worse than the baseline for: slow (<1/day) (116.7% vs 115.9%). For those series the simple rule is as good or better.
- At store-dept-day level the gain is small (9.2% vs 9.9%): averaging the same weekday is a strong baseline once SKUs are summed. The model earns its keep at SKU level, where orders are placed.
- Store-SKU-day WAPE is high for any method because most series sell 0-2 units a day.
- One origin and 28 days is one draw. The bootstrap covers series-to-series noise, not the chance that this particular month was easy or hard. Rolling-origin backtests are on the week-2 plan.
- P10 above the mean or P90 below it happened on 0.5% of predictions; those were clipped so P10 <= mean <= P90.
- This is not comparable to M5 leaderboard scores (different subset, horizon handling and metric).
